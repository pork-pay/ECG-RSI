ABSTRACT

Targeted waveform synthesis.
Feedback-driven model evolution.

Electrocardiogram (ECG) models need supervision that targets evolving weaknesses and remains faithful to waveform evidence. We introduce ECG-RSI, a recursive self-improvement framework combining verified waveform synthesis with feedback-guided intervention search. Signal remeasurement verifies waveform edits; construction errors guide generator repair, while learner errors motivate interventions compared on a separate selection set. Across seven benchmarks, three rounds improve Qwen3.5-35B-A3B by 21.47 percentage points in mean QA accuracy and 9.77 points in mean classification F1. Selected interventions shift from direct reinforcement to confusion-focused contrasts, suggesting that updates can change which supervision is most valuable next. Continued feedback correction yields diminishing external gains. RSI should therefore separate error diagnosis from intervention selection, renewing how it teaches as the learner evolves and assessing progress through external transfer.

01 — THE IDEA

Turn a missing capability
into a checkable training example.

ECG interpretation connects waveform evidence to measurements and clinical conclusions. More data alone do not ensure that a model learns the distinctions it is missing. The challenge is to construct specific ECG changes, consistent questions, and explanations grounded in the resulting signal.

ECG-RSI makes data construction responsive to the learner. Forward synthesis starts with an observed ECG. Reverse synthesis starts with a desired feature contrast—including an eligible distractor from a training question—and edits the waveform to realize that contrast before regenerating supervision.

Construct the evidence.
Verify the example. Learn from the feedback.

02 — THE METHOD

One data engine.
Two connected feedback loops.

SIGNAL → INSTRUCTION

Forward synthesis

Extract evidence from a real training-side waveform and generate aligned questions, answers, and explanations.

TARGET → SIGNAL → INSTRUCTION

Reverse synthesis

Translate a target into constrained waveform edits, remeasure the result, and construct supervision from achieved evidence.

01

Diagnose

Identify errors by capability, difficulty, and evidence representation.

02

Construct & verify

Synthesize candidates and repair rejected operators and prompts.

03

Train & compare

Train candidate branches from a shared parent with historical replay.

04

Select & repeat

Promote the winner and carry its accepted new questions forward.

Each winning round adds 10,000 previously unused accepted questions to the cumulative training set, alongside replay of all previous training data.

From an ECG feature request to an accepted example

A waveform delineator extracts candidate heart-rate, interval, and amplitude features. The parameter selector considers both extraction reliability and diagnostic utility. Reverse perturbation is applied only when the required features can be measured reliably in an eligible source.

For example, a PR-interval contrast requires measurable P onset and QRS onset. The edit must attain the requested interval range while preserving protected heart rate and QRS morphology within fixed tolerances. More complex disease patterns require their own verified morphology and clinical evidence.

S′ = Tδ(S)   →   u′ = f(S′)

The pipeline records target error and protected-feature drift separately. Checks cover continuity at edit boundaries, behavior outside the edit mask, lead alignment, and class-specific morphology. The source waveform and any donors are patient-disjoint from feedback, selection, and independent evaluation data.

Both synthesis routes then construct the question, compatible clinical context, answer, and explanation from the resulting evidence. A training-question distractor can suggest a new feature request, but its feasibility, applicability, and achieved signal must be checked before it becomes supervision.

A recipe adapts to the current learner

Each candidate data recipe jointly chooses a task or capability category, learner-relative difficulty, and supervision representation:

πta(g, d, s) = pta(g) · pta(d | g) · pta(s | g, d)

Difficulty is estimated by answering each training-side candidate repeatedly with the current learner under fixed decoding settings. Questions are grouped into reliably solved, mixed-success, and unresolved bins. An unresolved question is unresolved under this probe; it is not assumed to be intrinsically impossible.

Evidence-expanded supervision

Make necessary intermediate observations explicit. For a QRS-width error, identify onset and offset, convert grid distance to milliseconds, and check the diagnostic threshold using verified fiducials.

Error-targeted supervision

Add checks that address the observed failure. When measurements are correct but the diagnosis is wrong, add the relevant cross-lead differential evidence instead of a longer measurement explanation.

Original teacher explanations remain a third representation. Paired evidence expansion preserves the image, question, options, answer, and clinical facts. Token count and evidence-step coverage are recorded: longer explanations are not automatically better supervision.

Construction feedback repairs the right component

Failures are routed by cause. Target errors and boundary artifacts trigger operator repairs; missing morphology triggers applicability or source-retrieval changes; ambiguous questions trigger question/context repairs; unsupported explanations trigger rationale-prompt revisions.

Original and revised versions are compared on the same source signals, requests, random seeds, and attempt budgets, with fixed acceptance thresholds. Updates record their triggering feedback, changes, old and new versions, validation results, and next-round scope.

Same-parent branches test which intervention helps

Each round starts three branches from the same accepted parent. All branches preserve the previous cumulative training set and share the same historical replay manifest. Each adds 10,000 previously unused accepted questions and trains for three epochs.

BRANCH A

Direct reinforcement

Increase quotas for weak ECG categories and the question types exposing the deficit, while retaining related-category controls. An AV-block recipe can emphasize conduction measurements and diagnosis.

BRANCH B

Confusion contrast

Allocate questions to recurrent confusion pairs; construct options and explanations around their distinguishing ECG evidence. Regional STEMI contrasts, for example, require localization across leads.

BRANCH C

Difficulty & task curriculum

Use current-learner probes to reweight question types and difficulty levels, such as L3, multistep reasoning, or evidence-chain questions.

A fixed feedback set diagnoses errors, while a separate predesignated model-selection set determines promotion. The primary score, non-target regression tolerances, valid-output requirements, and stopping rule are registered before training. Only the best qualifying model and its new data continue the accepted lineage; if none qualifies, the original model is retained.

Uniform allocation and chain updates improve in earlier rounds but decline at R3. The tree trajectory continues to improve. Together with the construction ablation, these results show that both the composition of new data and the strategy used to retain updates affect subsequent performance.

What changes across RSI rounds?

Error diagnosis and intervention selection are distinct.

Feedback identifies gaps and motivates candidate interventions; training and candidate comparison determine which update is retained.

Reselect interventions as learner needs evolve.

The retained A–B–B path shifts from direct reinforcement to confusion-focused supervision. It is consistent with updates changing which training evidence is most useful next.

Local correction has diminishing external returns.

Feedback error rates fall by 5.80, 5.17, and 4.10 percentage points, while external S7 gains shrink to 13.07, 3.01, and 0.38 points. Later rounds warrant checks of transfer and regression.

Learner state and recipe content change together, so this trajectory does not isolate a causal effect of learner state or establish a universal learning progression. Fixed-target interventions compared across checkpoints would help distinguish these explanations. Diminishing gains alone do not identify their cause or an optimal stopping point.

07 — TRAINING & REPRODUCIBILITY

Ordinary supervised fine-tuning.
Feedback-driven data.

The learner trains on accepted image–question–response triples. Supervised responses include the explanation and answer, with optional clinical context. Replay and new-data proportions are tracked in training tokens as well as example counts.

The language model remains trainable, while the vision encoder and multimodal aligner are frozen. Candidate branches share the parent checkpoint and replay manifest so that their data interventions can be compared under the same development protocol.
